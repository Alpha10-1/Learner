## Why this matters at Tharisa

Everything the Genesis, Voyager and Vulcan plants process starts as rock that was drilled, blasted, loaded and hauled in the open pit. Capstone's Drilling and Load and Haul dashboards report that movement in metres, bank cubic metres (BCM) and tonnes. If you can't say which unit a tile uses, or where the material went, you can't tell whether a tonnage or strip-ratio figure is good news, bad news or a data problem.

## Learn

**The chain.** The mining dashboards follow material through a fixed sequence:

1. **Drill.** Holes are drilled in a *block*; blocks carry codes such as `1623_OBW_1170_01`. The Drilling Dashboard counts metres drilled, split into *fresh* metres and *re-drill* metres (holes drilled again), by pit (East, West, Far West) and by owner (Tharisa and contractor fleets).
2. **Blast.** The Open Pit Production Dashboard lists optimising drill and blast as one of its objectives. Blast records are not described further in the Capstone wiki; confirm with the Capstone team where they live.
3. **Load and haul.** Excavators load trucks. Each load is recorded with the truck, excavator, source, block, material class, material type and destination, plus loads, BCM and tonnes. Operators capture these hour by hour on the Load & Haul screen of the Mine Central app, and an administrator approves the entries.
4. **Destination.** The excavator table in the Load and Haul dashboard shows where each load went. In the wiki's example, ore loads go to *ROM – Genesis* or *ROM – Voyager*, the run-of-mine destinations named after the plants. Waste loads go to destinations such as *East pit permanent backfill*, *TSF 02* and *TSF 03*. The wiki does not say why waste goes to the TSF destinations; that is a good question for the Capstone team.

**Material classes.** Each load is classed as *bulk waste*, *interburden waste* (waste lying between ore layers) or *ore*. Ore is recorded by reef layer; MG2, MG3, MG4, MG4A and UG1 all appear on the dashboard.

**Three units for three questions.**

- **Metres** measure drilling effort.
- **BCM** measure rock volume as it lay in the ground, before it was broken. Waste is reported in BCM.
- **Tonnes** measure mass. Ore is reported in tonnes because plants are fed, and product is sold, by mass.

Converting BCM to tonnes means multiplying by a density (tonnes per BCM), and that factor is where many reporting surprises hide.

**Strip ratio** compares the waste moved with the ore exposed. A rising strip ratio means more hauling for every unit of ore that reaches the plants.

### Worked example: the strip ratio tile

The Load and Haul overview on page 3 of the wiki export shows Total BCM Mined 1,257,976, Total Waste BCM 1,161,048, Total Reef Tonnes 339,248 and Strip Ratio 11.98.

1. Ore volume = total BCM − waste BCM = 1,257,976 − 1,161,048 = **96,928 BCM**. The donut chart beside the tiles shows the same ore figure.
2. Waste BCM ÷ ore BCM = 1,161,048 ÷ 96,928 = **11.98**, which matches the tile. So this dashboard's strip ratio is volume over volume.
3. Waste BCM ÷ reef **tonnes** = 1,161,048 ÷ 339,248 = **3.42**. It looks plausible, but it mixes units and understates the waste burden by the density factor.
4. Reef tonnes ÷ ore BCM = 339,248 ÷ 96,928 ≈ **3.50** tonnes per BCM, the density the dashboard appears to apply.

Steps 2 and 4 infer the definitions from the numbers. Not documented in the Capstone wiki; confirm with the Capstone team: "Is strip ratio defined as waste BCM ÷ ore BCM, and which density converts ore BCM to tonnes?"

**A source quirk.** In the wiki's Load and Haul section, the description paragraph repeats the Drilling Dashboard text word for word. Rely on the Load and Haul screenshots and field names, not that paragraph.

## Do

Sandbox: your local copy of `docs/sources/capstone_data_platform_mining.pdf` and a calculator or Excel. Allow 12 minutes.

1. Open the PDF at page 3 and find the Load and Haul **Overview** row.
2. Write down four tiles: Total BCM Mined, Total Waste BCM, Total Reef Tonnes and Strip Ratio.
3. Calculate the ore BCM, then waste BCM ÷ ore BCM, then reef tonnes ÷ ore BCM.
4. Turn to page 4 and find the table under **Excavators**. For three rows, note the material type and the destination.
5. On one line, sketch the chain drill → blast → load → haul → destination, and write the unit each step is measured in.
6. Write one question for the Capstone team about something the dashboard doesn't explain.

**Expected result:** ore is about 96,928 BCM; the strip ratio is about 11.98, matching the tile; the implied density is about 3.50 tonnes per BCM. Ore rows go to ROM – Genesis or ROM – Voyager; bulk and interburden waste rows go to destinations such as TSF 02, TSF 03 or East pit permanent backfill. Your chain shows metres for drilling, BCM for material moved and tonnes for ore. A good question asks how strip ratio and density are defined, or why some waste goes to TSF destinations.

## Check

**Q:** Why is waste reported in BCM while ore is reported in tonnes, and what links the two?

**A:** BCM measures rock volume in the ground, which is what the fleet moves. Ore is reported by mass because plants are fed and product is sold by mass. A density factor in tonnes per BCM converts one to the other; about 3.5 is implied on the dashboard, but confirm the official value.

**Q:** The strip ratio tile reads 11.98. What does that mean physically, and why would dividing waste BCM by reef tonnes mislead you?

**A:** About 12 BCM of waste were moved for every 1 BCM of ore. Dividing by tonnes gives 3.42, which mixes volume with mass and understates the waste burden by the density factor.

**Q:** According to the Load and Haul dashboard, where do ore and waste loads end up?

**A:** Ore goes to the ROM destinations named after the Genesis and Voyager plants. Waste goes to destinations such as East pit permanent backfill and the TSF 02 and TSF 03 destinations. Why waste goes to TSF destinations isn't documented, so ask the Capstone team.
